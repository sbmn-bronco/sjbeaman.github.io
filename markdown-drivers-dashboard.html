Method, definitions and limits
Source
The attached Features data set: one row per store per week, 45 stores, February 2010 to July 2013.
Markdown spend
MarkDown1 through MarkDown5 summed for a store's week. Weeks where a store reported no markdown are left out of markdown statistics rather than counted as zero.
Pearson r
Strength of a straight-line relationship, from −1 to +1. As a rule of thumb, |r| under 0.1 is negligible, 0.1–0.3 weak, 0.3–0.5 moderate, above 0.5 strong. R² (r squared) is the share of variation one measure explains in the other.
Within store vs pooled
Within store removes each store's own average before correlating, so it measures how measures move together week to week inside a store. Pooled compares all store-weeks at once, which mixes in differences between regions (CPI, for example, sits near 130 for some stores and above 200 for others).
Partial correlation
The correlation that remains between two measures after removing the part each shares with a third (here, the holiday flag).
Confidence intervals and p-values
Correlation intervals use the Fisher z method; the holiday comparison uses Welch's t-test. Both treat each store-week as independent. Weeks from the same store and season are not fully independent, so true uncertainty is wider than shown: read small p-values as "clearly non-zero", not as exact probabilities. Measures that both drift over the period (markdowns eased from 2012 to 2013 while fuel rose) can correlate through that shared trend alone.
Holidays
The file's holiday flag, named by month: February is Super Bowl, September Labor Day, November Thanksgiving, December Christmas.
Left out
The file has no sales, so this page shows what goes with markdown spend, not whether markdowns pay off. Correlation does not establish cause.
